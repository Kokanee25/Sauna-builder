# 05 — Insulation & Vapor Barrier

This is what makes a sauna heat fast, hold heat, and not rot. Two systems:
**insulation** (slows heat loss) and the **foil vapor barrier** (blocks moisture
*and* reflects radiant heat back inside). Get the vapor barrier right — it's the
detail most DIY saunas get wrong.

## 1. Insulation

Use **mineral wool (rock wool)**, not fiberglass where you can — it tolerates
higher temperatures and doesn't sag. Fill every stud and ceiling cavity fully;
no gaps, no compression.

| Location | Target R-value | Reference product |
|----------|----------------|-------------------|
| Walls (2×4 cavity) | **R-13** | Mineral wool batt for 2×4 |
| Ceiling | **R-21 or higher** | Mineral wool batt for 2×6 (or doubled) |
| Floor (if framed/over unconditioned space) | R-13+ | As applicable |

**Why the ceiling gets more:** heat rises and the ceiling runs hottest, so
upgrading ceiling R-value gives the best return on heat retention.

> Wear a mask, gloves, and eye protection when cutting mineral wool.

## 2. The foil vapor barrier — the critical layer

After insulating, wrap the **entire interior** — all walls **and** the ceiling —
in a continuous **aluminum foil vapor barrier** on the **warm (interior) side**
of the insulation.

It does two jobs:

1. **Vapor/moisture block** — stops humid sauna air from driving into the
   insulation and framing, where it would condense, rot wood, and ruin R-value.
2. **Radiant reflector** — bounces radiant heat back into the room, so the sauna
   heats faster and holds temperature.

### Rules for the foil

- **Foil side faces the room** (the interior), reflecting heat inward.
- Run it **continuously** across walls and ceiling — the goal is an unbroken
  envelope.
- **Overlap seams ~2–4 in** and **tape every seam** with **aluminum foil tape**
  (not duct tape, not painter's tape — it must withstand heat).
- **Seal every penetration:** light cable, heater wiring, sensor cable, vents.
  Tape the foil tight around each, or use high-temp sealant.
- **Lap the ceiling foil over the wall foil** so any condensation runs down and
  out, not into a seam.
- Don't puncture it unnecessarily; staple at edges that will be overlapped/taped.

### Use the right foil

Use **pure aluminum foil** or **foil-faced sauna barrier** rated for high temp.
Do **not** use:

- Standard **kraft-faced fiberglass** as your only barrier (the paper isn't a
  proper reflective high-temp barrier).
- **Polyethylene (plastic) sheeting** — it can soften/melt and off-gas at sauna
  temperatures. Foil only.

## 3. The air gap (recommended)

After the foil, install **¼–¾ in furring strips (1×2)** vertically over the studs,
then nail the T&G paneling to the furring. This creates:

- A small **ventilated air gap** behind the paneling so any moisture that gets
  behind boards can dry, and
- A break that **protects the foil** from paneling fasteners and keeps the
  reflective surface working.

Run furring so paneling orientation works out (see finishing doc). Skipping the
gap is common in small builds but the gap improves longevity.

## 4. Wall build-up (inside → out)

```
SAUNA INTERIOR
  │
  ├─ Tongue-and-groove cedar paneling
  ├─ Air gap (1×2 furring)                ← optional but recommended
  ├─ Aluminum FOIL vapor barrier (foil → room), seams taped
  ├─ Mineral wool insulation (R-13 walls / R-21+ ceiling)
  ├─ Stud framing (2×4 @ 16" OC)
  └─ Existing wall / exterior sheathing
OUTSIDE
```

## 5. Insulation & barrier checklist

- [ ] All wall cavities fully filled, R-13 mineral wool, no gaps/compression
- [ ] Ceiling R-21+ (more than walls)
- [ ] Foil vapor barrier on warm side, **foil facing the room**
- [ ] Continuous across walls **and** ceiling
- [ ] All seams overlapped and **foil-taped**
- [ ] Ceiling foil laps over wall foil
- [ ] Every penetration (wires, vents, sensor) sealed
- [ ] No polyethylene used anywhere as the barrier
- [ ] Furring strips installed for air gap (recommended)
