# 08 — Ventilation

Good ventilation keeps oxygen up, distributes heat evenly, and helps the room dry
out after use (preventing mold and extending the wood's life). Bad ventilation
makes a sauna stuffy, unevenly heated, and short-lived. It's cheap to get right
and miserable to get wrong.

## 1. The principle: in low near the heater, out across the room

A sauna uses **convective airflow**: cool fresh air enters low near the heater,
gets heated and rises, circulates across the room, and stale/cooler air leaves
through an exhaust on the far side. You want a **diagonal sweep** of air across
the room, not a short-circuit from intake straight to exhaust.

```
   (exhaust high, adjustable) ───┐
                                 │   warm air rises, sweeps across
        ▲  ▲  ▲                  ▼
        │  │  │            ┌───────────┐
   ┌────┴──┴──┴────┐       │  exhaust   │  (low, opposite wall)
   │    HEATER     │       │   low      │
   └───────────────┘       └───────────┘
        ▲
   ┌────┴────┐
   │ INTAKE  │  fresh air in, low, near/under heater
   └─────────┘
```

## 2. Intake (supply)

- **Location:** low, **near or just below/behind the heater**, so incoming cool
  air is immediately drawn up over the stones and heated.
- **Size:** roughly match the exhaust; a common starting point is a vent of
  **~4 in (100 mm)** diameter/equivalent, scaled up for larger rooms.
- For electric heaters the intake is low. (For some setups intake is from the
  adjacent room; for outdoor builds it draws from outside.)

## 3. Exhaust (return)

- **Location:** on the **wall opposite the heater**, to pull air across the room.
- **Mechanical (forced)** ventilation: exhaust placed **lower** on the far wall
  works well, often ducted to an extract fan.
- **Natural (gravity)** ventilation: a common scheme is a **low exhaust** on the
  far wall PLUS a **separate high "drying" vent** that you open after use to dry
  the room. Configurations vary — pick one scheme and keep intake and exhaust
  roughly balanced.
- **Make it adjustable:** fit the exhaust with a **closable wooden slider/damper**
  so you can tune airflow during use and open it fully for drying afterward.

## 4. Sizing rule of thumb

- Provide roughly **equal intake and exhaust** area.
- A starting point for the reference 210 ft³ room: vents of **~4 in (100 mm)**
  each. Larger rooms scale up.
- Aim for several air changes per hour during use; an adjustable exhaust lets you
  dial it in by feel (if it's stuffy, open up; if it won't hold heat, close down).

## 5. The drying cycle (don't skip)

After every session: **open the exhaust/drying vent fully and leave the door
ajar** so the room dries out completely. Trapped moisture is the #1 enemy of a
wooden sauna's lifespan. This is the cheapest maintenance you'll ever do.

## 6. Coordinate with framing & barrier

- Frame the vent openings during [`docs/04`](04-framing.md).
- **Seal the foil vapor barrier** tightly around each vent sleeve
  ([`docs/05`](05-insulation-vapor-barrier.md)).
- Trim vents with **wood** covers/sliders, not plastic, inside the hot room.

## 7. Ventilation checklist

- [ ] Intake low, near/under the heater
- [ ] Exhaust on the wall opposite the heater (diagonal flow)
- [ ] Intake and exhaust areas roughly balanced
- [ ] Exhaust is adjustable (wooden slider/damper)
- [ ] Separate high drying vent OR a plan to open up + door ajar after use
- [ ] Foil barrier sealed around vent penetrations
- [ ] Wooden (not plastic) vent trim inside
