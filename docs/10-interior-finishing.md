# 10 — Interior Finishing

The visible, touchable sauna: paneling, benches, backrests, door, trim, and
accessories. Order of operations matters — **ceiling first, then walls, then
benches.**

## 1. Paneling (tongue-and-groove)

### Order

1. **Ceiling first.** Panel the ceiling before the walls so wall boards later
   tuck up under the ceiling edge and hide the joint.
2. **Walls next**, working around the room.

### Orientation: horizontal vs. vertical

| | Horizontal boards | Vertical boards |
|---|-------------------|-----------------|
| Look | Classic, makes room feel wider | Modern, makes room feel taller |
| Practicality | Easier to replace a damaged lower board; longer runs | Sheds water down the grooves |
| Furring direction | Furring runs vertically | Furring runs horizontally |

Either is fine. The reference build uses **horizontal cedar T&G** on the walls.
Match your furring direction to be perpendicular to the boards.

### Fastening

- **Blind-nail** through the tongue at each furring/stud with **stainless** finish
  nails/pins (16–18 ga, ring-shank) so no fastener head shows and no hot metal is
  exposed.
- Where face-nailing is unavoidable, **countersink and plug** or use stainless and
  set below the touch zone.
- Leave a small expansion gap at floor and corners; cover with trim.
- **Acclimate** the wood in/near the space for a few days before installing so it
  doesn't shrink and gap after the first heat.

### Finish

- **Leave interior wood bare**, or treat only with **sauna paraffin oil** on
  high-contact spots (benches, backrests, handrails). **No varnish/paint/poly.**

## 2. Benches

The most-touched part — use **clear (knot-free) cedar or aspen**.

### Geometry (reference)

| Bench | Height | Depth |
|-------|--------|-------|
| Upper | 36 in (915 mm) | 24 in (610 mm) |
| Lower | 18 in (460 mm) | 18 in (460 mm) |

### Construction

- Frame sturdy bench supports (2×4) anchored into the **wall backing you added
  during framing** ([`docs/04`](04-framing.md)) and/or to legs.
- Top with **slats** (2×2 or 1×4) spaced **~½ in (10–12 mm) apart** for drainage
  and airflow.
- **Round/ease all edges** and **countersink + plug** every screw (stainless),
  so no metal or sharp edge contacts skin.
- Leave a **gap under the lower bench** (open front) so air circulates and the
  floor dries.
- Make the upper bench (or its top) **removable** if you can, for cleaning/drying.

Detailed framing & spacing: [`blueprints/bench-detail.md`](../blueprints/bench-detail.md).

## 3. Backrests & headrests

- Mount **backrest slats** angled slightly off the wall so backs don't touch hot
  paneling and air flows behind.
- Optional shaped **headrests** on the upper bench.

## 4. Door

- Hang the **pre-hung outswing** door per its instructions; shim plumb; confirm
  the **ball/roller catch** (no locking latch).
- Wooden, non-conductive handles inside and out.
- Maintain the small under-door gap that aids ventilation.

## 5. Trim & details

- Cover corner joints and the floor edge with **cedar trim/molding**.
- Install the **heater guard rail** and the **light guard**.
- Mount the **thermometer/hygrometer** at upper-bench eye level on a wall away
  from the heater.
- Install the **duckboard** floor mat.
- Place the **bucket and ladle**; mount any towel hooks (wood) away from the
  heater.

## 6. Finishing order checklist

- [ ] Wood acclimated near the space
- [ ] Ceiling paneled first
- [ ] Walls paneled, blind-nailed with stainless, expansion gaps left
- [ ] Furring perpendicular to board direction (air gap maintained)
- [ ] Benches built from clear cedar/aspen, slats spaced, edges eased
- [ ] All fasteners stainless, countersunk + plugged
- [ ] Open space under lower bench; upper bench removable if possible
- [ ] Backrests/headrests mounted off the wall
- [ ] Outswing door hung, ball-catch (no lock), wood handles
- [ ] Trim, heater guard, light guard installed
- [ ] Thermometer/hygrometer, duckboard, bucket + ladle placed
- [ ] Interior wood left bare or only paraffin-oiled
